SOMA Health — Data Processing Agreement

Effective: August 23, 2026

This Data Processing Agreement ("DPA") forms part of the Master Service Agreement or Terms of Service (the "Agreement") between Soma Health Solutions Inc. ("SOMA," "Processor") and the customer entity that is signatory to the Agreement ("Customer," "Data Controller"). Capitalized terms not defined here have the meanings given in the Agreement or in applicable privacy law, as the context requires.

1. Scope, Roles, and Shared Responsibility

1.1 Roles

Customer acts as a Data Controller. SOMA acts as a Data Processor with respect to Patient Data and other Regulated Data the Customer entrusts to the Services.

1.2 Shared Responsibility Model

SOMA is responsible for the security and integrity of the Platform infrastructure and the managed services SOMA provides. Customer is responsible for its secure use of the Platform, including the protection of account credentials (e.g., strong passwords), the lawful basis for collecting and providing Regulated Data to SOMA, and the configuration of any Customer-controlled settings.

1.3 Bifurcation of Data

SOMA acts as a Processor for Patient PII and PHI. SOMA acts as a Controller for professional account information (such as names, credentials, billing contact, and login data) collected for account management, security, billing, and marketing purposes. SOMA’s collection and use of such professional account information as Controller is governed by SOMA’s Privacy Policy and is not subject to the terms of this DPA.

2. Processing and Instructions

2.1 Documented Instructions

SOMA shall process Regulated Data only on documented instructions from the Customer. The Agreement, this DPA, and the documented configuration of the Services together constitute the Customer's documented instructions.

2.2 Infringing Instructions

SOMA shall promptly notify the Customer if an instruction, in its opinion, infringes Canadian federal or provincial privacy legislation, the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (where applicable), or other applicable data-protection law. SOMA may suspend execution of the instruction until the matter is resolved.

2.3 Purpose Limitation and Restriction on Secondary Use

(a) Primary Purpose. SOMA shall process, store, use, and disclose Customer Regulated Data solely for the purpose of providing the Services to the Customer under the Agreement, including any technical operations reasonably necessary to support, secure, and maintain the Services.

(b) No Secondary Use. SOMA shall not use Customer Regulated Data, whether identified or de-identified, for any secondary purpose, including without limitation:

  1. training, fine-tuning, evaluating, or improving any artificial intelligence, machine-learning, or large-language model owned or operated by SOMA or any third party;
  2. creating aggregate datasets, benchmarks, or derivative products for use beyond the provision of Services to the Customer, except as expressly permitted under Section 2.3(c) below; or
  3. any commercial purpose external to the Customer’s use of the Services.

(c) Permitted De-identified Aggregate Use. Notwithstanding Section 2.3(b), SOMA may use properly de-identified, aggregated data derived from Customer Regulated Data (“De-identified Aggregate Data”) for the purpose of generating industry benchmarks, service-quality analytics, and product-improvement insights, provided that: (i) the de-identification is performed in accordance with recognized standards such that the data cannot reasonably be re-identified to any individual or to Customer; (ii) the resulting De-identified Aggregate Data does not identify or reasonably identify Customer; and (iii) the De-identified Aggregate Data is not used to train, fine-tune, or improve any artificial-intelligence or machine-learning model. If SOMA intends to use De-identified Aggregate Data for any purpose described in this Section 2.3(c), it will notify Customer in advance, and Customer may opt out by written notice to SOMA.

This restriction applies notwithstanding any contrary provision in the Agreement, the Terms of Service, or SOMA's Privacy Policy.

(d) Prior Written Authorization Required. Any proposed use of Customer Regulated Data outside the scope of Section 2.3(a) requires the Customer's prior written authorization. The Customer may grant, withhold, condition, or revoke such authorization in its sole discretion. Silence does not constitute authorization.

(e) Sub-processor AI Confirmation. SOMA confirms that no sub-processor that processes Regulated Data uses such data to train, fine-tune, or improve any model, dataset, or commercial product. All artificial-intelligence sub-processor relationships operate under zero-data-retention configurations and executed Business Associate Agreements or Data Processing Addendums, as set out in Annex II and Section 5. For the avoidance of doubt, this restriction expressly applies to audio data, transcripts, and any derivative outputs generated through the Session Recording feature, all of which constitute Regulated Data subject to this Section.

(f) Permitted Operational Use. Nothing in this Section restricts SOMA's ability to perform technical operations reasonably necessary to deliver, secure, support, troubleshoot, or maintain the Services, including: monitoring for abuse or security threats; request routing and load balancing; backup and disaster recovery; aggregated, non-identifying operational metrics (such as system uptime, error rates, and latency) that do not contain or derive from Regulated Data content; and lawful compliance with regulatory or legal obligations.

3. Security and Breach Notification

3.1 Technical Measures

SOMA implements technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risks presented by the processing, including AES-256 encryption at rest, TLS 1.2 or higher in transit, enforced multi-factor authentication for administrative access, role-based access controls with least-privilege defaults, and logging and monitoring of administrative and security-relevant events.

3.2 Compliance Program

SOMA maintains an information-security program appropriate to the nature and sensitivity of the Regulated Data processed. SOMA does not currently hold an independent third-party security certification. PIPEDA and applicable provincial health-privacy legislation are statutory obligations, not certifications. The parties may revisit this Section if SOMA obtains a third-party certification during the term.

3.3 Breach Notification

SOMA shall notify the Customer of any Regulated Data Breach or DPA Security Incident without undue delay and, in any event, within seventy-two (72) hours of becoming aware of it. The notification will include, to the extent then known, a description of the nature of the incident, the categories and approximate number of records and data subjects affected, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed to address it.

3.4 Session Recording Processing

Where the Customer enables the Session Recording feature:

(a) Audio in Transit. Audio is transmitted via TLS to a zero-retention transcription endpoint operated by SOMA or by an authorised transcription sub-processor (as identified in Annex II), transcribed in real time, and discarded at the transcription tier without persistent storage. SOMA does not retain raw audio recordings at rest in its infrastructure.

(b) Transcript Storage. Transcripts and any AI-generated summaries derived from Session Recording are stored at rest within Canada in accordance with Section 10 (Data Residency), encrypted with AES-256, and subject to the deletion provisions in Section 8.

(c) Access Restrictions. Access to transcripts and Session Recording derivatives is limited to:

  1. Customer users authorised by the Customer's account administrators; and
  2. SOMA personnel performing narrowly defined incident response, security investigation, or technical support functions, in accordance with SOMA's documented access-control policies.

4. Government Request Transparency (Schrems II)

4.1 Redirection

If SOMA receives a legally binding request from a public authority (such as a law-enforcement or government agency) for access to Customer Data, SOMA will, where lawfully permitted, attempt to redirect the authority to the Customer.

4.2 Notification

Unless legally prohibited, SOMA will notify the Customer of any such demand to allow the Customer to seek a protective order or other appropriate remedy.

4.3 Challenging Requests

SOMA will review and, where there are reasonable grounds, challenge any demand for Customer Data that is over-broad or unlawful under applicable law, particularly where such a demand conflicts with applicable Canadian or U.S. data-protection obligations.

5. Sub-processors

5.1 Authorization

The Customer grants general authorisation for the sub-processors listed in Annex II.

5.2 Notice and Objection

SOMA shall provide thirty (30) days' notice of any change to sub-processors that process Regulated Data. The Customer has fifteen (15) days to object on reasonable data-protection grounds via privacy@soma-health.ca. If an objection cannot be resolved within a further reasonable period, the Customer may terminate the affected Services.

5.3 Sub-processor Obligations

SOMA shall not engage any sub-processor (including, for clarity, any provider of artificial-intelligence, machine-learning, or large-language-model services) for the processing of Regulated Data unless that sub-processor has entered into a written agreement with SOMA containing data-protection, security, and confidentiality obligations no less protective than those SOMA has undertaken to the Customer under this DPA, the Agreement, and any executed Business Associate Agreement. SOMA confirms that, as of the Effective Date, all sub-processors processing Regulated Data have executed such agreements, including a Business Associate Agreement or Data Processing Addendum where the sub-processor processes Protected Health Information. SOMA shall remain liable to the Customer for the acts and omissions of its sub-processors to the same extent as if performed by SOMA itself.

6. International Transfers

6.1 Data Residency

See Section 10 below. Customer Regulated Data is stored at rest in Canada.

6.2 Transfer Mechanism

Customer warrants that, as of the Effective Date, no Regulated Data provided to SOMA is subject to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) or the UK GDPR. If Customer’s circumstances change such that any Regulated Data may include personal data of data subjects in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, Customer will notify SOMA in writing before providing such data. Upon such notice, the parties will negotiate and execute the appropriate transfer mechanism (such as the European Commission Standard Contractual Clauses (Module 2) or the UK International Data Transfer Addendum) before any such processing begins, and SOMA reserves the right to suspend processing of such data until that mechanism is in place.

7. Audits and Compliance Assistance

7.1 Audit Reports

The Customer's right to audit is primarily satisfied by SOMA providing its Security Pack, which includes SOMA's current security overview, architecture summary, sub-processor list, data-flow description, and other supporting compliance documentation as available. SOMA does not currently hold an independent third-party security certification; if SOMA obtains such a certification during the term, the related report will be added to the Security Pack and made available to the Customer on request under a written confidentiality undertaking. The composition of the Security Pack may evolve over time.

7.2 Audits

If the Security Pack is insufficient to demonstrate compliance, the Customer may conduct a focused audit. Such audits must be:

  1. requested with reasonable notice;
  2. conducted during business hours;
  3. subject to a strict Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA); and
  4. performed at the Customer's sole expense.

8. Deletion and Return

8.1 Post-Termination Deletion

SOMA shall automatically and permanently delete all Patient Data (PII and PHI) within ninety (90) days of contract termination, unless retention is required by law. Patient Data subject to this Section includes transcripts and AI-generated derivatives produced through the Session Recording feature. Audio recordings are not retained by SOMA and therefore are not subject to a post-termination deletion period.

8.2 Verification

Written certification of deletion is available to the Customer upon request.

9. Liability and Governing Law

9.1 Liability Cap

Each party's total aggregate liability arising out of or related to this DPA shall be subject to, and counted toward, the limitations of liability set out in the Agreement, including any super cap for data-breach claims set out therein. The carve-outs in Section 9.2 below apply notwithstanding any such cap.

9.2 Carve-outs

Notwithstanding Section 9.1, nothing in this DPA limits or excludes either party's liability for:

  1. fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation;
  2. wilful misconduct or gross negligence;
  3. liability that cannot be limited or excluded under applicable law, including under applicable data-protection laws; or
  4. a party's indemnification obligations under the Agreement.

9.3 Governing Law and Jurisdiction

This DPA shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the Province of Newfoundland and Labrador and the federal laws of Canada applicable therein. The parties submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of St. John’s, Newfoundland and Labrador for any dispute arising out of or related to this DPA.

9.4 Conflict with the Agreement

In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Section 9 and the liability or governing-law provisions of the Agreement:

  1. the liability carve-outs set out in Section 9.2 shall apply notwithstanding any limitation or cap on liability in the Agreement, including any super cap for data-breach claims; and
  2. the governing-law and jurisdiction provisions set out in Section 9.3 shall control with respect to any dispute arising out of or related to this DPA, the processing of Regulated Data, or the obligations of either party under applicable privacy laws.

10. Data Residency

10.1 Storage Location

SOMA attests that Customer Regulated Information and Protected Health Information ("Regulated Data") is stored at rest within Canada, as follows:

  1. operational database and managed-service data: Supabase in Canada (Central) — AWS ca-central-1, Montreal;
  2. object storage, computed artefacts, transcripts, and AI-generated derivatives: Amazon Web Services ca-central-1, Montreal.

SOMA maintains a Canadian production environment and provisions each Customer’s tenant into that environment. SOMA may update the specific storage locations listed in this Section 10.1 to reflect changes in infrastructure, provided that: (i) any replacement or additional location remains within Canada; (ii) SOMA provides Customer with at least thirty (30) days’ prior written notice of any such change; and (iii) the updated location is reflected in a revised version of this DPA made available to Customer.

10.2 Customer Responsibility and Changes

The Customer is solely responsible for the accuracy of any jurisdictional information provided at sign-up and for keeping that information current. Any request relating to data residency must be submitted to privacy@soma-health.ca. Where the Customer's regulatory profile requires additional data-residency restrictions beyond those committed in this Section, the Customer should raise the requirement before contract execution.

10.3 Scope

This attestation applies to Regulated Data at rest. Ancillary processing necessary to operate the Services, including transient processing by zero-retention sub-processors operating under executed Business Associate Agreements and Data Processing Addenda, is governed by the remainder of this DPA, and in particular by Sections 2.3 and 5.

11. Patient Consent and Lawful Basis for Processing

11.1 General Lawful Basis

The Customer represents and warrants that, prior to providing any Patient Data to SOMA — whether by uploading clinical documents, providing patient identifiers, generating clinical notes, requesting AI-assisted drafting, enabling any feature that creates or transmits Patient Data, or otherwise — the Customer has obtained all consents and holds all other lawful authority required under applicable law for SOMA to process that Patient Data on the Customer's behalf in accordance with this DPA. The Customer is responsible for ensuring that its instructions to SOMA fall within the scope of the consents and authorities the Customer has obtained.

11.2 Session Recording — Additional Warranties

Where the Customer enables the Session Recording feature, the warranty in Section 11.1 expressly includes the additional consents required from the data subject (the patient, or the patient's authorised representative in the case of minors or incapacitated individuals) for the recording, transcription, and processing of session audio under all applicable laws, including but not limited to: the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and applicable U.S. state laws governing recording and consent (including two-party-consent jurisdictions); and the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and applicable provincial health-privacy laws.

11.3 Documentation

The Customer is solely responsible for obtaining, documenting, and maintaining records of patient consent and other lawful authority. SOMA's in-product confirmation mechanisms (including any pre-session consent acknowledgement) are operational tools provided for the Customer's convenience and do not transfer the underlying legal obligation from the Customer to SOMA.

11.4 Indemnification

The Customer shall indemnify SOMA against any claims, damages, fines, or regulatory actions arising from the Customer's failure to obtain or maintain valid consent or other lawful basis for SOMA's processing of any Patient Data on the Customer's behalf, including but not limited to consent for Session Recording.

11.5 Suspension Right

SOMA reserves the right to suspend or restrict the Customer's, or any specific user's, access to the Services or to any individual feature (including but not limited to Session Recording) if SOMA has a reasonable basis to believe the Customer is processing Patient Data in violation of applicable consent or lawful-basis requirements. SOMA will provide written notice and, where feasible, an opportunity to cure prior to suspension.

Annex I — Details of Processing

Subject Matter. SaaS psychological assessment and clinical-reporting platform.

Duration. For the term of the Agreement, plus the post-termination retention period set out in Section 8.

Nature and Purpose. Delivery of the Services described in the Agreement, including hosting, storage, retrieval, transmission, AI-assisted drafting, transcription, and related operations.

Data Subjects. Licensed psychologists and allied healthcare professionals, their authorised support staff, and their patients (including minors).

Data Categories. Special-category health data, assessment results, clinical notes, identifying PII, and — where the Customer enables the Session Recording feature — transcripts of patient sessions and any AI-generated summaries or clinical observations derived from such transcripts. Audio processed through Session Recording is transcribed in transit and discarded; SOMA does not retain audio at rest.
